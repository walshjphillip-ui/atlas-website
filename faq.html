General
What is Atlas?

Atlas is a personal AI advisor for ambitious students — primarily those in finance, economics, and business. It lives in Telegram and texts you proactively: a personalized morning brief, market alerts, deadline reminders, and follow-up nudges.

It's also a tool you can talk to: ask market questions, prep for interviews, draft cold emails, track applications, and log networking contacts. Unlike general AI tools, Atlas builds a profile of you over time and tailors everything around your actual goals.

How is Atlas different from just using ChatGPT?

ChatGPT and similar tools are general-purpose: they wait for you to ask something and answer without any knowledge of who you are or what you're working toward.

Atlas is different in three ways:

  • It knows you — your school, major, career goals, applications, contacts, and watchlist are all part of how it responds
  • It texts you first — morning briefs, alerts, and reminders are pushed to you, not waiting for you to log in
  • It takes actions — saves Gmail drafts, tracks applications, logs contacts, sets reminders, watches stock prices

Use ChatGPT for homework, essays, and general questions. Use Atlas for everything career and market related.

How much does it cost?

Atlas is completely free during the beta. No credit card required. No trial period that auto-converts to paid.

It's in beta now, which means features are still being added and some things may change. The goal is to keep it free or low-cost for students long-term. If that ever changes, existing users will get advance notice.

What does "beta" mean in practice?

It means Atlas is functional and being used by real students, but it's still being actively developed. New features are added regularly. Occasionally something may not work perfectly — if you hit a bug, just tell Atlas directly and it gets flagged.

Being in beta also means your feedback actually shapes the product. If something is annoying or a feature is missing, say so.

Telegram
Why does Atlas use Telegram instead of its own app?

Two reasons. First, you already check your messages. Atlas goes where you already are — you don't have to remember to open a separate app.

Second, Atlas is built to text you proactively. Morning briefs, market alerts, and reminders are push notifications that arrive in your messages — the same way a text from a real person does. That wouldn't work in a web app or ChatGPT-style interface.

What is Telegram? Is it safe?

Telegram is a messaging app used by over 900 million people worldwide. Think WhatsApp or iMessage — except it's not owned by Meta or Google, and it doesn't run ads or sell your data to advertisers.

All messages are encrypted in transit. Telegram doesn't read your messages or share them with third parties for advertising. Your Telegram account is private — Atlas only sees messages you send directly to it.

Does Atlas have access to my other Telegram conversations?

No. Atlas is a Telegram bot — it can only see messages sent directly to it in your conversation with Atlas. It has no access to your other chats, groups, contacts, or any other data on your Telegram account. Telegram's bot API is designed this way.

I don't have Telegram. What do I do?

Download it free from the App Store or Google Play. Sign up with your phone number — same process as WhatsApp. It takes about 60 seconds. Then tap the "Open in Telegram" button above and you're in.

Gmail & Google
Does Atlas read my emails?

No. Atlas only has permission to create drafts. It cannot read, access, or interact with any emails in your inbox, sent folder, or anywhere else in your Gmail account.

When you connect Gmail, you go through Google's standard OAuth flow and grant only the specific permission needed: gmail.compose. That permission lets Atlas write a draft and save it. Nothing else.

Can Atlas send emails on my behalf?

No. Atlas saves drafts. You review the draft, decide if you want to send it, and hit send yourself. Atlas never sends mail without your explicit action. This is a deliberate design choice — every email that goes out is reviewed by you first.

What exactly does Gmail access allow?

The only Gmail permission Atlas requests is gmail.compose, which is the most restricted Gmail scope available. Here's exactly what it does and doesn't allow:

  • ✓ Create drafts in your Gmail drafts folder
  • ✗ Cannot read any existing emails
  • ✗ Cannot send emails
  • ✗ Cannot delete emails
  • ✗ Cannot access your contacts
  • ✗ Cannot access Google Drive, Calendar, or any other Google service

You can verify this yourself by going to myaccount.google.com/permissions after connecting — it shows exactly what permissions any connected app has.

How do I disconnect Gmail?

Two ways. Either tell Atlas "disconnect my Gmail" and it handles it, or go directly to myaccount.google.com/permissions and revoke access there. Either method immediately removes Atlas's ability to create drafts.

Is Gmail connection required to use Atlas?

No. Gmail is entirely optional. Every feature — morning briefs, interview prep, application tracking, market data, reminders — works without it. Connecting Gmail just adds the ability to save cold email drafts directly to your inbox instead of copying and pasting them yourself.

Privacy & Data
What data does Atlas store about me?

Atlas stores what you tell it: your name, school, major, career goal, interests, hometown, watchlist, market sector preferences, applications, contacts, and reminders. It also stores notes from your conversations that are relevant to your profile — things like "told me they have a Goldman first round" or "interested in restructuring."

It does not store your Telegram messages verbatim. It stores the profile information and structured data (applications, contacts, reminders) that you explicitly provide.

Is my data sold to third parties?

No. Your data is never sold, shared with advertisers, or used to target you with ads. Atlas is a tool — your data exists to make Atlas more useful to you, not to be monetized.

How do I delete my data?

Tell Atlas "delete my account and all my data" — it will remove your profile, applications, contacts, reminders, and memory notes from the database entirely. If you want confirmation or have trouble, you can also email the address in the privacy policy.

Who built Atlas and who has access to my data?

Atlas was built by Phillip Walsh, an Economics student at the University of Tampa. It's an independent project. User data is stored in a secured database (Supabase) with row-level security enabled. No one else has access to your individual data.

See the About page for more on who built it and why, and the Privacy Policy for the full technical breakdown.

Features
What time does the morning brief arrive?

The brief is sent in the early morning — typically before 8 AM. The exact timing may vary slightly. You can also request it manually at any time by typing /brief.

Can I update my profile after onboarding?

Yes. Just tell Atlas. "Change my career goal to private equity," "add AAPL to my watchlist," "update my major to Finance" — it handles natural language updates and saves them immediately.

How does the interview prep work?

Tell Atlas which firm and round you're prepping for: "prep me for my Goldman first round" or "practice Blackstone case questions." It will ask you questions in the format expected by that firm, evaluate your answers, and give model responses for anything you get wrong.

It covers technicals (DCF, LBO, accounting), behavioral questions, market knowledge, and firm-specific questions. The format changes based on whether it's a first round, superday, or case interview.

Does Atlas track my applications across sessions?

Yes. Applications are stored in your profile, not just in the conversation. If you say "I got a first round at Goldman," Atlas saves it. The next day it appears in your morning brief. You can view your full tracker anytime by saying "show my applications."

Can Atlas set reminders?

Yes. Tell it "remind me to submit my Citi application on May 12th" or "remind me about my Goldman prep session tomorrow at 3 PM." The reminder fires at the specified time as a Telegram message. Upcoming reminders are also surfaced in your morning brief.

Account
Do I need to create an account?

No. Your Telegram account is your identity. When you open Atlas and answer the onboarding questions, your profile is created automatically. No email signup, no password, no separate account to manage.

What happens if I lose access to my Telegram account?

Your Atlas profile is linked to your Telegram user ID. If you recover your Telegram account (same phone number), Atlas will recognize you. If you get a new phone number, your profile won't carry over automatically — contact via the privacy policy email to sort it out manually.

How do I stop receiving messages from Atlas?

You can mute notifications for the Atlas conversation in Telegram settings without losing your profile, or you can type "stop sending briefs" to pause the morning brief specifically. To stop everything and delete your data, say "delete my account."

Still have questions?

Atlas is built by one person, not a company with a 5-day support queue. If something isn't clear or something isn't working, you can ask Atlas directly in the chat — or reach out via the contact listed in the privacy policy.
